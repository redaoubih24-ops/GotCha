# Second Opinion figures

These four editable SVGs were drawn for GotCha in 2026. The vector paths and styling are original figure adaptations or simple geometry, distributed under the repository's MIT license. They contain no external images, fonts, scripts, filters, downloaded artwork, or logos.

- `vase-faces.svg`: a Rubin-style figure/ground construction. One shared contour bounds a cream vase and two facing black profiles; the image does not switch between separate drawings.
- `necker-cube.svg`: a geometric Necker construction, made from two equal squares and four connectors. Every edge has the same stroke; no shading, dashed lines, or occlusion chooses the nearer face.
- `duck-rabbit.svg`: a new simplified contour of the classic duck/rabbit construction. There is one head and one eye. The same two leftward extensions serve as an open bill or long ears. No scan was traced or embedded.
- `circle.svg`: an original circle made from simple SVG geometry. This fourth image is deliberately obvious: both “Circle” and “Definitely a circle” are valid answers. No external reference was needed.

All use a `600 × 400` viewBox and a cream/ink palette. For the first three figures, preserve the shared boundaries and uniform wireframe edges when editing: adding facial detail, perspective shading, or separate objects can remove the ambiguity. Keep the fourth image an unmistakable circle, with no competing shapes or hidden interpretation.

The game rejects one answer on each of the first three figures and immediately advances, keeping a correction explicitly associated with the previous image visible. It then accepts either answer on the circle; there are three corrections and four images in total.

## Construction references

These sources were consulted to check the reversible constructions and their historical context; their image files are not bundled or copied:

- [Joseph Jastrow, “The Mind's Eye,” Popular Science Monthly, January 1899](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Popular_Science_Monthly/Volume_54/January_1899/The_Mind%27s_Eye). The original article discusses reversible wireframe figures and includes the duck/rabbit figure.
- [Commons record for the duck/rabbit figure from that 1899 article](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PSM_V54_D328_Optical_illusion_of_a_duck_or_a_rabbit_head_%28cropped%29.png), identifying its source and U.S. public-domain status. GotCha uses newly authored curves rather than that scan.
- [Ian Remsen's Rubin-vase construction record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Two_silhouette_profile_or_a_white_vase.svg), a CC0 example of the shared-boundary principle. GotCha's profile paths are independently drawn.
- [Stevo-88's Necker-cube construction record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Necker%27s_cube.svg), a public-domain example. GotCha's squares and connectors are independently specified.
